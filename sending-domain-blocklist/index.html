Is your sending domain on a blocklist?

By Alexander Rosales · Updated 10 November 2026

In November 2026 I checked 356 sending domains used for cold email. 130 of them, 36%, were on SURBL or Spamhaus DBL. Most of their owners didn't know. Here is what a listing does to your email, how to check your own domains, and how to get one removed.

What a domain blocklist is

SURBL and the Spamhaus Domain Blocklist (DBL) are lists of domains, not of servers. A domain lands on them when it shows up in mail that people or spam traps report as unwanted.

Spam filters look up every domain they find in a message: the address you send from, the links in the body, the tracking domain behind them, the domain in your signature. One listed domain is enough to push the whole email toward spam.

A listing follows the domain wherever it's written

I learned this on my own mail. I wrote to 1,012 companies about their sending domains. In 128 of those emails I named a domain of theirs that was on a blocklist. Two of those recipients wrote back to say my email had landed in their spam folder, one on Zoho and one on Google Workspace. None of the other 884 said so.

Two cases is a small number, and I treat it as one. But it matches how these lists work: the filter doesn't care who wrote the domain, only that it's there. So a listed domain hurts replies, forwards, and any email from a clean domain that links to it or mentions it.

How to check your sending domains

  1. Write down every domain you send from, plus your tracking domain and any domain in your links or signature.
  2. Look each one up on the lists' own pages: SURBL's lookup and Spamhaus's checker. Both are free.
  3. Check again a week later. A domain that is clean today can be listed after one bad campaign.

Authentication is a separate question. A domain can pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC and still be listed. The free checker on this site covers authentication; the lookups above cover the lists.

What to do when one is listed

  1. Stop sending from it today. Take its inboxes out of every campaign. Each new email extends the listing.
  2. Find out why. The usual causes are bounces from an unverified list, too many emails per inbox per day, or spam complaints from a campaign that reached the wrong people.
  3. Ask for removal on the list's own page. SURBL and Spamhaus both have a removal request next to the lookup result. Neither one charges for it. Anyone who asks you to pay for delisting is not the list.
  4. Move the campaign to a backup domain that is already warm. If you don't have one, your outreach stops for the two to three weeks a new domain needs.
  5. Keep the listed domain out of your other mail. Not in a signature, not in a link, not in a redirect, until it is off the list.

How to avoid the next listing

  • Verify every list and leave catch-all addresses out. In my own campaigns they bounced at 4.19%, against 1.09% for verified addresses.
  • Keep the volume per inbox low and steady.
  • Always have one or two backup domains warming, so a listing costs you a day and not three weeks.
  • Write down the age of each domain. In one audit, sending domains lasted two to three months before they started burning.
